# Hazmat Handling Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines the rules for handling hazardous material containers at Meridian Port Authority.

The purpose of this policy is to ensure the safe handling, storage, transportation, inspection, and release of hazardous cargo while protecting port personnel, the environment, and surrounding facilities. The policy also establishes standardized operational procedures to reduce risks associated with dangerous goods and to ensure compliance with national and international safety regulations.

---

## Rules

1. Any container marked as hazardous material (hazmat = true) requires additional verification before release.

2. Hazardous containers must not be released without approval from an authorized supervisor.

3. If a hazardous container also has an active customs hold, customs approval is required before any release action.

4. Transportation companies handling hazardous containers must have an active status.

5. All hazardous material movements must be recorded in the system for auditing purposes.

6. Hazardous containers must always be stored in designated hazardous cargo zones.

7. Containers with damaged packaging must be isolated immediately until a safety inspection has been completed.

8. Hazardous cargo must never be transported without the required safety documentation.

9. Every hazardous shipment must have an assigned emergency contact available throughout the transportation process.

10. Unauthorized personnel are not permitted to enter hazardous cargo storage areas.

---

## Hazard Classification

Hazardous materials should be classified according to their hazard type.

Typical classifications include:

- Explosives
- Flammable Liquids
- Flammable Solids
- Toxic Materials
- Corrosive Materials
- Oxidizing Agents
- Radioactive Materials
- Compressed Gases
- Miscellaneous Dangerous Goods

Each classification may require different storage conditions, handling procedures, and emergency response actions.

---

## Release Requirements

Before releasing a hazardous container, verify:

- Container identification.
- Hazard classification.
- Customs status.
- Carrier authorization.
- Required approvals.
- Safety inspection results.
- Supervisor approval.
- Dangerous Goods Declaration.
- Emergency contact information.
- Storage location verification.

The release process must stop immediately if any required information is missing or invalid.

---

## Inspection Procedure

Before approving the release of hazardous cargo, inspectors should complete the following steps:

1. Verify the container identification number.
2. Confirm the hazard classification.
3. Inspect the external condition of the container.
4. Check for leakage or visible damage.
5. Verify all required documentation.
6. Confirm customs clearance status.
7. Review previous inspection records.
8. Record inspection results in the system.
9. Obtain supervisor approval before final release.

No hazardous container may be released until every inspection step has been completed successfully.

---

## Storage Requirements

Hazardous containers must be stored according to the following requirements:

- Store hazardous cargo only in approved hazardous storage areas.
- Separate incompatible hazardous materials.
- Maintain safe distances between hazardous containers.
- Ensure fire protection systems remain operational.
- Keep emergency equipment accessible at all times.
- Monitor storage areas regularly for safety risks.

Containers that fail safety inspections must remain isolated until corrective actions have been completed.

---

## Transportation Requirements

Before transportation begins, verify that:

- The carrier is authorized to transport hazardous cargo.
- The transport vehicle satisfies safety requirements.
- Drivers possess the required certifications.
- Emergency response information accompanies the shipment.
- All regulatory documentation has been approved.

Transportation should not begin until every safety requirement has been verified.

---

## Emergency Case

If a hazardous container has both:

- Active customs hold.
- Hazardous material flag.

The release request must be paused until human approval is provided.

The request must also be suspended if:

- A leak is detected.
- Fire or explosion risk exists.
- Required documentation is missing.
- Safety inspection fails.
- Customs authorities request additional investigation.

Manual approval is mandatory before processing can continue.

---

## Incident Reporting

Every hazardous material incident should be recorded with the following information:

- Container Number
- Hazard Classification
- Date and Time
- Location
- Officer ID
- Description of the Incident
- Corrective Actions
- Final Resolution

Incident reports should remain available for future investigations and regulatory audits.

---

## Audit Logging

Every hazardous cargo operation must record:

- Officer ID
- Supervisor ID
- Timestamp
- Container Number
- Hazard Classification
- Inspection Result
- Approval Decision
- Release Status
- Notes
- Emergency Actions (if applicable)

Audit records must remain available for safety reviews and compliance inspections.

---

## Exception Handling

The following situations require manual review:

- Damaged hazardous containers.
- Containers with conflicting inspection reports.
- Missing safety documentation.
- Expired transportation permits.
- Active customs investigations.
- Hazardous cargo involved in security incidents.

Automated processing must stop until all issues have been reviewed and approved by authorized personnel.

---

## Compliance Requirements

All hazardous material operations must comply with applicable port regulations and international dangerous goods handling standards.

The hazardous cargo management system should:

- Prevent unauthorized releases.
- Maintain complete inspection records.
- Record every approval and rejection.
- Track hazardous cargo movements.
- Support regulatory audits.
- Protect operational safety.

---

## Record Retention

The system should retain:

- Hazardous cargo records
- Inspection reports
- Supervisor approvals
- Incident reports
- Audit logs
- Supporting documentation
- Emergency response records

Records should remain available according to the organization's document retention policy.

---

## Example Scenarios

### Scenario 1

A hazardous container has completed inspection and all required approvals have been received.

**Result:** The supervisor may authorize the release after recording the decision in the audit log.

### Scenario 2

A hazardous container has an active customs hold.

**Result:** The release request remains suspended until customs approval has been granted.

### Scenario 3

A damaged hazardous container is discovered during inspection.

**Result:** The container is moved to an isolation area, and emergency inspection procedures begin immediately.

### Scenario 4

A transportation company attempting to collect hazardous cargo has an inactive operating status.

**Result:** The release request is rejected until the carrier becomes authorized again.
